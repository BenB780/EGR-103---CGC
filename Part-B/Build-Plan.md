# Part B: Build Plan

## Prototype concept

The proposed prototype is a cardboard grain carrier designed as a shallow box with reinforced side walls and a top handle. The carrier is sized to hold a small amount of grain while remaining lightweight, easy to carry, and simple to build from flat cardboard stock.

### Concept schematic

The concept consists of:
- a rectangular base tray approximately 18 in long x 10 in wide
- side walls approximately 8 in tall
- a front lip and rear lip for rigidity
- a recessed carry handle cut into the top panel
- corner gussets and internal support tabs to increase strength

Key dimensions:
- Overall length: 18 in
- Overall width: 10 in
- Overall height: 8 in
- Wall thickness: 0.5 in (structural fold allowance)
- Handle opening: 5 in wide x 2.5 in tall

See the accompanying concept schematic image in `Part-B/concept-schematic.svg`.

## Technical schematic and dimensions

The prototype is built from a single flattened cardboard layout with fold lines and cut paths marked clearly. The layout is based on the following panel arrangement:

- Main bottom/base panel: 18 in x 10 in
- Front and back wall panels: 18 in x 8 in (2 panels)
- Left and right wall panels: 10 in x 8 in (2 panels)
- Top handle panel: 10 in x 2 in (reinforced as a carry band)
- Corner tabs: 1 in x 1 in tabs on each corner for structural locking
- Fold flanges: 0.5 in on all attachment edges

### Planned cut/fold pattern

- Solid lines = cut lines
- Dashed lines = fold lines
- Small rectangles at panel corners = glue or tab joints

All dimensions are nominal and intended for a prototype using cardboard sheet stock. Adjust spacing to match the actual cardboard thickness and tape/glue method used in the build.

## Build task order

1. Gather materials and tools.
   - Corrugated cardboard sheets
   - Utility knife or box cutter
   - Metal ruler or straight edge
   - Pencil, marker, and tape
   - Glue or hot glue
   - Cutting mat or protected surface

2. Transfer the technical schematic onto the cardboard.
   - Mark all cut lines, fold lines, and reinforcement tabs.
   - Confirm all dimensions are aligned with the final concept.

3. Cut the cardboard pieces.
   - Cut the base, side panels, front/back panels, and handle reinforcement.
   - Leave the fold tabs intact.

4. Score the fold lines.
   - Use a ruler and a blunt edge to create clean folds without tearing the cardboard.
   - Score along all dashed fold lines before assembly.

5. Assemble the sides.
   - Fold the front and back panels upward.
   - Attach the side panels to create the rectangular carrier body.

6. Add the base and reinforcement tabs.
   - Secure the bottom panel and reinforce corners with tabs.
   - Check that the box is square and stable.

7. Install the handle and top support.
   - Cut the handle opening in the top section if required.
   - Reinforce the top rim with extra cardboard strips.

8. Add structural bracing.
   - Tape or glue gussets at the corners and along the base edges.
   - Reinforce the handle area to support load.

9. Test the prototype.
   - Fill the carrier with a small amount of grain or equivalent weight.
   - Check for bending, loose joints, or instability.

10. Refine the prototype.
   - Adjust dimensions, add more braces, or improve the handle if necessary.
   - Finalize the prototype for presentation and documentation.
